When Is Tracking Affordable? A Movement-Budget Feasibility Threshold for Dynamic Regret

Ridham PatelIndian Institute of Technology Gandhinagar
Read PDF ↗OpenReview ↗

Abstract

Dynamic regret measures an online learner against a moving comparator, and standard analyses assume the learner may move freely at every round. We study what happens when it may not: the learner is given a hard budget S on its total movement ∑ₜ ‖xₜ − xₜ₋₁‖. Our main result is that a movement budget creates a feasibility barrier. On a domain of diameter D with G-Lipschitz convex losses, for every comparator path length Pₜ ∈ [D, DT] we exhibit a deterministic adversary that forces dynamic regret = Ω(GDT) on every budget-feasible algorithm whenever S ≤ cPₜ for a universal constant c > 0. This barrier is caused by the budget rather than by a volatile environment: for fixed D, it applies when Pₜ = o(T), where the unconstrained minimax rate Θ(G√(D(D + Pₜ)T)) is sublinear, so the same instances are learnable with an unbounded budget and unlearnable without one. Conversely, projected online gradient descent with a budget-aware step size is budget-feasible and attains o(GDT) once S = ω(D + Pₜ). Thus the critical movement scale is D + Pₜ: linear regret is unavoidable below a sufficiently small constant multiple of Pₜ, while sublinear regret is achievable when S/(D + Pₜ) → ∞ in the learnable regime. The construction rests on a distinction we make explicit: what makes a comparator expensive to track under a movement budget is the distance it forces the learner to travel, not its path length. Above the threshold we give a budget-dependent upper bound and identify the tight minimax rate in this region as an open problem rather than claiming a product law. Experiments support the predicted transition location and the 1/S decay.
